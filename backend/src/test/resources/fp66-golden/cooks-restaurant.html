Will AI replace Cooks, Restaurant?

Not entirely. Today's AI tools can take on about 13% of the typical work in this occupation. None of the largest tasks is highly automatable; AI mostly helps around the core work. The parts that stay human include: Tasting and seasoning by experience; Timing many dishes at once in a rush; Keeping a station clean and safe.

People who learn to use AI for the automatable parts keep the edge. The 3-phase plan below shows where to start.

A 3-phase AI learning plan

Phase 1

Use the kitchen tech that saves time

  • Learn your kitchen display system and order flow so tickets, timings and modifications never get lost.
  • Use digital temperature probes and food safety apps that log readings automatically, and act on every alert.
  • Ask an AI assistant to scale recipes, convert units or suggest substitutes, and test the result before service.

Project

Switch your station's temperature and cleaning checks to a digital log for a month and compare missed checks with paper.

Phase 2

Less waste, better prep

  • Use prep lists based on expected covers from the POS, and adjust them with what you know about the day.
  • Track food waste at your station for a few weeks and change portions or prep amounts where waste is high.
  • Write down your recipes and techniques clearly with AI help, so new cooks can learn your station faster.

Project

Measure food waste at your station for two weeks, change two prep amounts, and measure again for two weeks.

Phase 3

Grow your cooking and leadership

  • Keep building taste, timing and technique; that is what makes a cook valuable in any kitchen.
  • Learn to lead a station or a shift: teaching, staying calm and keeping quality up in a rush.
  • Leading a station and training cooks prepares you for sous chef and head cook roles, and catering uses the same skills at larger scale.

Project

Train one new cook on your station using your written recipes, and check their dishes against yours after two weeks.
